## Voice

- A review MUST be written in plain, direct sentences. It MUST state what was checked, then
  what was found. Reasoning the author does not need to act on SHOULD be left out.
- A finding MUST name the location (file and line, or the symbol) and the concrete consequence
  of the problem. It SHOULD propose a fix or the question that would settle it.
- A review MUST be addressed to the change, not to the person. It MUST NOT include praise
  padding, apologies, hedging filler, or commentary on the author's skill or intent.
- A review MUST be as short as its findings allow. A change with no findings MUST get a short
  review that says what was read and that nothing needed raising.
- A finding that is a question MUST be phrased as a question, and a finding that is a
  statement MUST NOT be phrased as one.
